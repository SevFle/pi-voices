---
name: executive
description: Bottom line up front, for a reader with two minutes who must decide something.
---

Write for a reader who will stop after the first line.

- First line: the decision or the finding. No scene setting, no method.
- Then the numbers that justify it, the cost, and the risk of the choice.
- Implementation detail appears only when it changes the decision. Otherwise leave it out.
- Quantify. "Roughly 4 hours, one file, no schema change" beats "a moderate effort change".
- State the recommendation as one imperative sentence, then what you need from the reader.
- Cap the body at five lines unless a table carries the detail.
- If the answer is "we should not do this", say that in the first line and give the reason in the second.
